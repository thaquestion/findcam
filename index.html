Pacing

How fast does Cam run?

Cam runs 10 to 11 minute miles over all terrain. He doesn't walk, no matter the elevation, the climb or the grade. The only exception is his first quarter mile of the day. He stays that consistent all day long.

Can he slow down for me?

Normally he'd happily run any pace you like. For this effort, he needs to hold his pace, so plan to keep up with 10 to 11 minute miles for as long as you're out there.

Good to know

First and foremost, Cam is so excited to have you. He values nothing more than human connection, and people are the highlight of his journeys. Everything below is here to give you as much time on your feet with him as possible and to make the experience great for you. It all comes with love.

Will he wait while I get ready?

No. Cam's day is planned to the minute, so he leaves each stop right away. Arrive with your vest, belt and shoes sorted, or you'll be playing catch-up.

What happens at the 4-mile stops?

He refuels and sometimes changes clothes. Those stops are tightly timed and he knows exactly how long each one takes. Just roll with the vibes.

Can the crew help me out?

Please be self-sufficient. The crew's job is to keep Cam moving and fed all day, which keeps him on schedule and stacking up miles.

Cam looks pretty beat up. Is he okay?

Running 75 miles a day for six weeks takes a real toll, so he might look worn down, walk a little stiff, have a bloody nose, or have some tape and bandages. That comes with an effort like this, and his team keeps a close eye on his health. Please don't offer medical advice or suggest fixes. The best thing you can bring him is good company and good energy.

Can I wait in the van during breaks?

No, the van is for Cam and his crew. Bring clothes to stay warm and dry while you wait.

Please don't

Cam's crew are some of the most helpful people you'll ever meet, but everything they carry is measured out for this run. Space is tight, so nothing extra is kept on hand. With that in mind, please don't ask the crew for any of these:

  • Rides
  • Storage
  • Food
  • Water
  • Shelter
  • Clothing
  • Supplements

If there's time, the crew can take a photo for you. Please don't interrupt the media team while they're shooting photos or video.

Before you head out

  • Check his live Garmin map right before you leave.
  • Park well off the road at a gas station, church lot or park, never on the shoulder ahead of him.
  • Bring your own water and food so the crew doesn't have to look after you.
  • After dark, wear a headlamp and something reflective.

How the estimate works

From his last GPS ping, the rest of today uses today's average pace including stops. Later days start at 5 am local from the planned day starts, use each day's terrain-adjusted pace, and follow his usual slowdown late in the day, built from his last seven days of two-hour splits. The window widens about half an hour for every day further out, since small daily differences add up. Today's route in Iowa is the exact course. Past Osceola the route is drawn town to town between the planned day finishes, so the closest point can be off by a few miles until the full route file is added.
